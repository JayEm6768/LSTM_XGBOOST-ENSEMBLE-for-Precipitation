Model Scorecard

Test set: 1,860 days (Apr 2014–Dec 2020), windowed to match the baseline study exactly. Reproduced baseline LSTM: RMSE 6.2009 in.

RMSE & MAE — average error, in inches

Ensemble RMSE clears the baseline by 9.2%, just under the 10% target — but its MAE sits above the baseline, not below it.

  • RMSE — average error in inches, with large misses weighted more heavily (each error is squared before averaging). Best: 0 in. No ceiling — always worse the higher it goes.
  • MAE — average error in inches, every day weighted equally regardless of size. Best: 0 in. No ceiling — reflects a “typical” day's error better than RMSE does.

MAPE — error as % of actual rainfall

All three models read far above the baseline; XGBoost's percentage error is worst despite having the lowest RMSE.

  • MAPE — average error expressed as a percent of that day's actual rainfall. Best: 0%. No fixed ceiling — a small miss on a nearly-dry day can spike this number, which is why it reads so high here.

Goodness of fit — R², Pearson r, explained variance

All three sit at or below zero: none of these models explain more variance than simply predicting the historical mean every day.

  • R² — share of rainfall's variation the model accounts for. Best: 1.0 (perfect). 0 = no better than always guessing the average; below 0 (seen here) = worse than that.
  • Pearson r — how closely predictions track the true ups and downs, −1 to 1. Best: 1.0. 0 = no relationship; negative = moves opposite to reality.
  • Explained Variance — like R², but ignores any constant over/under-shoot in the predictions. Best: 1.0. ≤0 = no real explanatory power.

Prediction bias — mean error, pred − true

Every model under-predicts on average; LSTM's bias is roughly 20× larger than XGBoost's.

  • Bias — average of (prediction − actual), in inches. Best: 0 in (no systematic drift). Negative = under-predicts rain on average; positive = over-predicts — neither direction is “less wrong” than the other.

Improvement vs. baseline, by metric

Only RMSE improves across the board. MAE and MAPE get worse for XGBoost and the ensemble — RMSE-based selection optimizes the mean, not the typical case.

  • Improvement % — how much lower a metric is than the reproduced baseline LSTM, as a percent. Best: 100% (metric driven to zero). 0% = tied with baseline; negative (red) = worse than baseline, not just “less improved.”

Error by rainfall intensity

LSTM wins decisively on dry days; XGBoost is worst on dry days and best on heavy rain — the ensemble lands between the two everywhere.

  • MAE by intensity — the same MAE as above (average error in inches), computed separately for dry / moderate / heavy-rain days. Best: 0 in in every bucket. Shows whether a model's overall accuracy comes from doing well everywhere, or just from the easy dry days.

Residual spread — typical vs. worst-case miss

All three models carry a catastrophic single-day miss (−78 to −80 in) that dominates RMSE far more than it dominates MAE.

  • Residual — (prediction − actual) for every test day, in inches. The solid box is the typical spread (mean ± 1 std dev); the thin whisker is the full worst-to-best range. Best: a narrow box centered on 0 with a short whisker. A whisker much longer than the box flags rare catastrophic misses hiding among many small ones.
